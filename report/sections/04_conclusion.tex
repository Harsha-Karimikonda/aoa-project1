\section{Conclusion}
In this report, we examined two foundational algorithmic strategies applied to real-world applied computing challenges: greedy interval scheduling for satellite payload management, and divide-and-conquer closest-pair detection for air traffic collision avoidance. For both problems, we formulated rigorous mathematical abstractions, derived asymptotic complexities, proved correctness through formal inductive methods, translated the algorithmic mechanics back into domain concepts, and verified performance via comprehensive empirical benchmarking. The experimental findings confirmed the theoretical $\mathcal{O}(n \log n)$ bounds across diverse scaling orders, proving the operational efficacy of both paradigms.
